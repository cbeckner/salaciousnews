SalaciousNews has learned that the collapse of one of Silicon Valley’s most towering titans is far more salacious than a simple corporate misstep. Jensen Huang, the recently ousted CEO of Nvidia, isn’t just facing congressional scrutiny over exports—he’s being hauled before lawmakers to answer for a tangled web of alleged affairs, one with a woman suspected of being a Chinese intelligence asset. This isn’t a boardroom drama; it’s an international spy thriller playing out in the hushed corridors of power, and it’s vaporized half a trillion dollars in market value in a mere five days.

Huang’s stunning fall began with a meek admission of being “unfaithful” to his wife of 40 years following reports of a relationship with Alice Huang (no relation), a Singaporean executive under U.S. suspicion of being a Chinese spy. But the plot, as they say, thickened. This week, explosive reports revealed a second, clandestine office romance with a long-time Nvidia executive, Kimberly Powell, who reported directly to the CEO. Sources whisper that employees noticed the pair were “unusually close,” painting a picture of a corporate culture where the lines between professional and personal were not just blurred, but erased.

The scandal has sent shockwaves from Cupertino to Capitol Hill. Senator Tim Scott has summoned Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify next month, declaring “serious concerns about our national security.” Three House committees are also demanding a closed-door interview. The question on everyone’s lips: Did pillow talk compromise the crown jewels of American tech? Nvidia’s own investigation claims “no reason to believe” secrets were spilled, but the very need for the probe speaks volumes.

Huang’s personal implosion has triggered a corporate catastrophe. The architect of the AI revolution, whose chips power the world’s most advanced technology, is now a pariah. Interim CEO Colette Kress is left picking up the pieces of a company whose stock just suffered its worst five-day plunge since going public. Employees who became millionaires on paper watching their shares soar are now under strict orders not to speak to the press, trapped in a golden cage of their own making.

The saga exposes the perilous intersection of unchecked executive power, personal vice, and geopolitical tension. It’s a cautionary tale for the ages: a man who built a trillion-dollar empire from the ground up watched it crumble not from market forces, but from secrets whispered in the dark. The American public, and Congress, are now demanding to know just how deep this rabbit hole goes.
