Locked away in a simple black leather diary, the late Pope Francis confessed to spiritual battles so intense they would make an exorcist blanch. In a bombshell book compiled by his closest aide, the world now learns the pontiff was tormented by frequent 'demonic attacks,' including three terrifying encounters with the devil himself. The Prince of Darkness, according to Francis's own hand, possessed 'eyes of fire' that were both 'menacing' and 'seductive.' In one harrowing 2018 episode, after a trip to Chile scarred by abuse scandals, the Pope heard the devil's voice—a 'powerful raspiness'—boasting, 'Now I've gotten everything I wanted.' This wasn't a metaphorical struggle; this was a visceral, supernatural war played out in the very halls of the Vatican.

The diaries, spanning from his time as a bishop in Argentina to his final years on the Throne of St. Peter, peel back the humble facade to reveal a man in profound crisis. Between 2021 and 2022, Francis endured a spiritual meltdown tied directly to the 'curiosity' of power. He grappled with feelings of 'self-sufficiency' and the temptation to treat others 'like puppets,' a path he described as leading him 'to the gates of hell.' He even peeked into the inferno itself in a dream, a vision of 'pagan seduction in full force' that terrified him so profoundly he grabbed his rosary 'as a weapon.'

In a stunning display of self-restraint—or perhaps spiritual warfare—Francis refused to purge his critics from the Vatican's powerful Curia, even when bishops begged him to fire saboteurs. He explained he felt a 'strong sensation' that God forbade him from becoming a 'puppeteer.' Instead, he embraced a stark humility, ditching papal palaces for a simple guesthouse, swapping fine robes for orthopedic shoes, all in a desperate bid to dismantle 'every symbol of papal majesty.'

The revelations don't stop at the supernatural. The diaries offer a raw, unvarnished look at his papal regrets, particularly his initial, disastrous mishandling of Chile's clerical abuse crisis. He admits he 'fell off the cot'—a jarring Argentine phrase meaning he was rudely awakened to the severity of the scandal. The book also drops tantalizing crumbs about his successor, Leo XIV, whom Francis called 'irreproachable,' and offers surprisingly charitable words for his fiercest critics within the College of Cardinals.

This is more than a historical document; it's a portrait of a soul under siege, battling the darkest forces imaginable from the epicenter of the Catholic world. The Pope who preached simplicity was haunted by complexity, a man who saw the devil's eyes in the choir loft and heard his voice in the meeting room. The diaries don't just reveal a crisis of faith; they expose a breathtaking battle between light and darkness, fought not with swords, but with a worn rosary and a steadfast, trembling heart.
