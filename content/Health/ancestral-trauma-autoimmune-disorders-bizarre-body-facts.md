You thought your family was just quirky? Think again, sweetheart. The skeletons in your ancestral closet aren't just metaphorical — they're biological time bombs, and they're exploding in your immune system right now. That's the bombshell 'science' sweeping the internet, where armchair experts are claiming the trauma your great-great-grandma endured can manifest as crippling autoimmune disorders in YOU. It's the ultimate generational curse, served with a side of bizarre body 'facts' that sound ripped from a late-night horror podcast.

Let's unpack this juicy gossip, shall we? One viral thread had the audacity to declare that teeth aren't bones. The outrage! The dental drama! People were absolutely fuming, clutching their pearls and their molars. But hold your horses — and your hydroxyapatite — because apparently, it's true. Teeth are imposters in the skeletal system, the ultimate biological betrayals. They're harder, they don't regenerate like bones, and they're basically the drama queens of the calcium world.

Then there's the star of the show: peristalsis. That's the wave-like muscle motion that pushes your lunch through your gut, not, we repeat NOT, the magical mechanism that lovingly tucks your organs back into their anatomical beds after a wild night out. The internet had it all wrong, folks. Your intestines aren't a tidy little housekeeper; they're a muscular subway system, and your dinner is the late-night train. This revelation alone has shaken the very foundation of 'gut feelings' everywhere.

But back to the main event: intergenerational trauma as a health hazard. It's the gossip that keeps on giving. Proponents whisper about epigenetic changes — how experiences can alter how genes are expressed, not the genes themselves — potentially passing down a predisposition for stress-related illnesses. It's the ultimate 'blame it on the ancestors' excuse. Anxiety? Thank your Victorian-era relatives. Unexplained inflammation? A gift from your great-grandpa who survived the war. It's a narrative so deliciously scandalous, it practically writes our headlines for us. Is it all just pseudoscientific hokum, or is there a kernel of truth buried under generations of family drama? The jury's still out, but the chatter is absolutely deafening.
