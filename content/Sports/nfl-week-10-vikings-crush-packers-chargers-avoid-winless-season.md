The gridiron gods delivered a Sunday of pure, unadulterated schadenfreude, topped by the Minnesota Vikings dancing on the grave of their bitter rivals. Fresh off stealing quarterback Kirk Cousins, the Vikings didn't just beat the Green Bay Packers at Lambeau Field—they utterly humiliated them, 30-10, completing a season sweep of historic proportions. This marks the first time since the Reagan era that the Packers have been beaten by 17+ points twice in one season by the same team. The message? A new, brutal sheriff is in the NFC North, and his name is Kevin O’Connell.

Meanwhile, in Kansas City, Patrick Mahomes decided to remind the league he doesn’t need a running game to eviscerate defenses. In a dazzling display of ‘remember me?’ energy, Mahomes unleashed two deep bombs—ending a 20-game drought of long touchdowns—and tossed five TD passes total in a 35-13 rout of Atlanta. It was a masterclass in quarterbacking vengeance, a stark warning that the Chiefs’ offensive woes were merely a temporary, and now concluded, nightmare.

The real soap opera, however, unfolded in Los Angeles, where the hapless Chargers somehow, against all cosmic odds, managed not to fumble away a victory. Securing their first win of the season in a white-knuckle 24-21 finish over the Giants, the Chargers avoided the ultimate embarrassment of a winless season start. The relief on the sideline was palpable, a mixture of sheer exhaustion and disbelief that the football gods had finally spared them for one week.

And let’s not forget the dramatics in Cleveland, where Deshaun Watson—amidst his own perpetual off-field controversies—engineered yet another fourth-quarter comeback, joining a list of QB legends with a game-winning drive in each of his team’s first six wins. It’s the kind of stat that fuels endless debate: villain or hero? On the field, at least this week, he was the latter. The NFL continues to be a league where statements are made not with whispers, but with seismic hits and shocking scoreboards.
