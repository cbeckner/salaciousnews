In a bombshell interview that’s shaking the foundations of celebrity scandal, Mia Latter—once known only as ‘Alice’—has thrown open the doors to a nightmare from the naughties, naming names and calling for a revolution. The 37-year-old alleges Russell Brand, then 30 and a king of the MTV castle, referred to her as ‘the child’ in front of his entourage, a chilling moniker that speaks volumes about the grotesque power imbalance she claims defined their relationship. She was just 16, she says, a schoolgirl he allegedly ordered to keep their intimacy a secret from her friends.

Latter’s story, first aired as part of the explosive 2023 Times investigation, has hardened. What was initially described as a sexual assault she now calls rape, a brutal alleged act that came towards the end of their time together. While Brand was recently sentenced to a decade behind bars for other crimes, Latter’s case remains in the grim limbo of an ongoing Met Police investigation, a process she has now stepped away from to wage a different war.

Her weapon of choice? A crusade to change the law. Latter is demanding a ‘staggered’ age of consent, a legal shield to protect teenagers from the predatory advances of adults in their thirties, forties, and beyond. ‘It should be a criminal offence,’ she declares, her voice cutting through the decades of normalized sleaze where famous men preyed on young girls as a twisted rite of passage. Cabinet Office Minister Louise Haigh, reacting to the proposal, called the culture that celebrated such relationships ‘disgusting,’ but stopped short of a full endorsement, saying the idea ‘deserves proper consideration.’

The subtext is a scandal all its own: a systemic failure that allowed powerful men to operate with impunity in plain sight. Latter’s harrowing account of being a teenage secret, dubbed ‘the child’ by her much older alleged abuser, paints a picture of a bygone era that wasn’t so bygone after all. It’s a story of whispered instructions, hidden visits, and a celebrity machine that looked the other way. Her decision to waive anonymity and step into the spotlight is a direct challenge to that old world order, a demand for accountability that extends beyond a single courtroom and into the very fabric of the law. The question now isn't just about what happened then, but how many other ‘children’ were told to keep quiet.
