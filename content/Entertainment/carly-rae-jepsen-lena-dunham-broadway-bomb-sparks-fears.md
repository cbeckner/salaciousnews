Broadway is bracing for impact. In a theatrical pairing more baffling than a last-minute plot twist, pop princess Carly Rae Jepsen and professional provocateur Lena Dunham are teaming up to bring '10 Things I Hate About You' to the Great White Way. The musical adaptation of the beloved 1999 teen rom-com — a sacred text for a generation that still swoons at Heath Ledger's stadium serenade — has set a start date of August 17, 2027, and the theater district is already buzzing with a potent mix of curiosity and sheer, unadulterated terror.

Let's dissect this potential disaster, shall we? Jepsen, of "Call Me Maybe" fame, is writing the music and lyrics with Ethan Gruska. While her tunes are undeniably catchy, the leap from synth-pop bangers to a full-fledged Broadway score is a canyon, not a step. Meanwhile, Dunham, the polarizing creator of 'Girls,' is co-writing the book. Her signature brand of messy, hyper-specific millennial angst feels worlds away from the Shakespearean-tinged, paintball-fight charm of the original film. Can she capture the heart of Kat Stratford without making her insufferable? That is the million-dollar question haunting investors' dreams.

Adding to the high-wire act is the directorial choice: Christopher Wheeldon, the choreographic genius behind 'MJ' and 'An American in Paris.' His elegant, classical style seems an odd fit for a story about high school rebels and staged dates. The production is brazenly skipping the traditional out-of-town tryout, opting instead for a single nerve-wracking public presentation in New York before the full Broadway plunge. It's a move that screams either blinding confidence or catastrophic hubris.

The original film is a cult classic for a reason. It launched the careers of Ledger, Julia Stiles, and Joseph Gordon-Levitt, wrapping sharp wit and genuine heart in a 90s plaid package. This stage version risks being a hollow, nostalgia-baiting cash grab, a fate worse than a botched poetry slam performance. With no casting announced and no theater secured, the entire venture feels like it's being built on a foundation of pure vibes and questionable choices.

Will this be a surprise hit that marries Jepsen's earworms with a fresh take on teenage rebellion? Or will it be a trainwreck of epic proportions, a two-and-a-half-hour cringe fest that makes audiences long for the simpler times of mixed tapes and Heath Ledger's smile? Only time, and the brutal New York critics, will tell. But one thing's for sure: all eyes will be on this potential Broadway bloodbath.
