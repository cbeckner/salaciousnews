It was a showdown straight out of a biblical epic, but this time, the Goliath was wearing a Guardians of the Galaxy T-shirt. James Gunn and Peter Safran, the once-untouchable kings of DC Studios, thought they had survived Hollywood’s latest merger massacre. They were plotting a third Superman film, basking in the modest glow of a cheap horror hit, and feeling secure in their power. Then, Skydance’s co-CEOs David Ellison and Ynon Kreiz arrived like a pair of angelic avengers for a fateful visit to the Warner Bros. lot. Sources whisper that the atmosphere turned ice-cold faster than a Kryptonian winter.

The message was brutal and final: despite previous promises, their reign was over. In a twist worthy of a Shakespearean tragedy, their replacement wasn't a slick Hollywood insider or a corporate shark. It was Jeremy Latcham, a mild-mannered, faith-focused producer who had once been instrumental in Gunn’s own Marvel ascent. Talk about a Judas kiss! Latcham, who left the Marvel machine to make inspirational movies about the Wright Brothers and biblical kings in Texas, is now the chosen one to cleanse the House of DC.

Insiders paint a picture of a palace coup simmering for months. Gunn and Safran, accustomed to absolute power and reporting directly to the top, chafed against the new Skydance motion picture heads, Josh Greenstein and Dana Goldberg. The DC duo were reportedly slow to share plans and footage, clinging to their fiefdom. But the real sin, in the eyes of the new money-men? The bottom line. ‘Supergirl’ was a financial disaster, and even the ‘profitable’ Superman didn’t have the cultural ‘pop’ Skydance craves.

Enter Latcham, described as a ‘down-the-middle shooter’ whose goal is to make hits for the masses—your grandma, not just the Comic-Con crowd. Where Gunn trafficked in f-bombs and obscure characters, Latcham’s mission is to deliver family-friendly, broadly appealing spectacles aligned with Ellison’s tastes. It’s a total philosophical purge. Gunn posted a relieved farewell on Instagram, claiming he’s happy to escape the executive politics, but the sting of this divine intervention is surely felt. The question now: will any of his grand DC vision survive, or is the entire universe being rebooted for a more… heavenly audience?
