The 'Hollywood Madam' is back, but this time, her scandal doesn't involve A-list clients or black books—it features a flock of squawking, free-flying parrots. Heidi Fleiss, the notorious former procurer to the stars, is now locked in a bitter legal showdown with Nevada's Clark County Animal Protection Services, and the feathers are flying in more ways than one.

According to explosive court documents, Fleiss was slapped with a $500 fine after a neighbor, driven to despair, complained that Heidi's colorful companions were using telephone wires as their personal runway, unleashing a dawn chorus of 'squawking' before the civilized hour of 6 AM. The nerve! For Fleiss, this isn't about a nuisance; it's a fundamental fight for avian liberty. She passionately argued in court that her birds have a God-given right to fly, demanding the fines be tossed out and claiming the law itself is for the birds.

In a stunning admission that reads like a line from a surrealist play, government lawyers actually conceded, 'It is true that birds fly and they are probably happier and healthier when they fly free.' A win for Heidi? Not so fast. The bureaucrats fired back with a devastatingly pedantic point: these aren't wild spirits of the sky. They are captured, imported pets, living in gilded captivity on Heidi's Nevada compound. Their freedom, the state implied, is just another one of Fleiss's elaborate illusions.

The case lays bare a battle of philosophies: the unbridled, libertine spirit of Old Hollywood, embodied by Fleiss, versus the tidy, rule-bound world of municipal government. It's a clash between the desire for untamed, natural beauty and the neighbor's sacred right to sleep in. Is Heidi a crusader for creature comfort, or merely a woman whose chaotic past has simply gone to the birds?

As the legal proceedings continue, one can only imagine the scene: a former madam, once whispered about in the most exclusive circles, now standing before a judge arguing parrot parole. It's a tale so bizarre, so perfectly Heidi, that it proves truth is always stranger—and more scandalous—than fiction. The question remains: who will rule the roost?
