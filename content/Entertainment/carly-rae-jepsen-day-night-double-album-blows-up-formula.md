Hold onto your earbuds, pop music stans, because Carly Rae Jepsen has officially lost her MIND. The queen of writing 200 songs for a 12-track album has now unleashed a staggering 24-track, double-album monstrosity titled 'Day and Night,' and the results are as chaotic, excessive, and utterly, wonderfully unhinged as the star's personal life. Fresh off a whirlwind romance and a pregnancy glow, CRJ isn't just serving synth-pop anymore—she's serving a whole buffet of psychedelic detours, bossa nova flirtations, and disco-drenched confessions, all while pondering her controlling mother and her own impending parenthood. Talk about a creative baby bump!

The source tells SalaciousNews exclusively that this isn't your older sister's 'Call Me Maybe' energy. Gone is the pure, sugary rush. In its place? A sprawling, sometimes sagging, sonic experiment. For 'Day,' think wistful ballads and folk-tinged dreams where Jepsen's voice is all breathy urgency, whispering about domestic bliss in a one-bedroom apartment. But when the sun sets for 'Night,' she transforms into a dance-floor siren, crafting ABBA-level homages and clubby synth bangers that beg the question: is this love, or is it the hormones?

Insiders close to the 'Dedicated' singer reveal the album is soaked in the scent of new love and marital security, written as she fell for and married musician-producer Cole M.G.N., who also lent his talents to the project. By the mixing stage, she was reportedly pregnant, adding a layer of profound, trippy joy to tracks that veer from painfully vulnerable confessions ('I'm controlling like my mother') to aggressively cheery family chants ('We go hard in this family!'). It's a wild ride, and not every hook is razor-sharp—some say the second halves of both 'Day' and 'Night' drag like a reluctant walk down the aisle.

But let's be honest: restraint was never Jepsen's brand. She's the artist who will repeat 'really' six times in a chorus to make sure you GET it. So, is a 24-track, loosely conceptual double album a masterstroke or a mess? The critics are whispering about 'unwieldy' sprawls and 'left-field details,' but the fans are eating it up. This is CRJ unleashed, unafraid, and utterly immersed in the most psychedelic experiment of all: committed love and creating life. The video for 'After All,' featuring a gloriously pregnant Jepsen dancing in a sun-drenched apartment, says it all. It's surreal, it's joyous, and it's the most scandalously authentic thing pop music has seen in years.
