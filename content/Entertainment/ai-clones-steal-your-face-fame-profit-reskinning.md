Forget deepfakes. The hottest—and most infuriating—trend online is ‘reskinning,’ where soulless AI doppelgängers are brazenly pilfering the talents, jokes, and dance moves of real human creators. These digital vampires, often crafted as hypersexualized ‘conventionally attractive’ bots, are amassing millions of views and followers by doing nothing more than stealing someone else’s viral moment and slapping on a new, algorithm-pleasing face.

Take Sophie Lloyd, a guitarist who rocks stadiums with Machine Gun Kelly. She’s watched in horror as her hard-earned shredding performances are stolen by an AI-generated siren named ‘Mila Vasari,’ who strums a phantom guitar with distorted fingers to Lloyd’s exact music and movements. The Vasari account, a front for a Fanvue adult content page, is just one of countless digital parasites cashing in on stolen creativity. ‘They’re getting thousands of followers for literally doing nothing and stealing the stuff that we’ve created,’ Lloyd fumes.

From Norwegian tea-dancing sensation Kjetil Krogstad, who was cloned into a viral bunny, to British car enthusiast Grace Roberts, who found her jeans-and-T-shirt persona transformed into a blonde in a mini-dress, the victims are everywhere. Comedians are seeing their meticulously crafted jokes performed by AI avatars, costing them crucial exposure and gigs. It’s a hustle-culture nightmare where originality is obsolete, and success is a simple ‘Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V’ away.

Behind these eerily perfect avatars lies a grim reality: cheap AI tools and a ruthless drive for monetization. The accounts peddle everything from adult subscriptions to shady crypto coins, all built on the pilfered personalities of real people. Legal protections are laughably behind the curve, leaving creators playing a futile game of digital whack-a-mole. As one exposer of AI fakes, Alwyn Cruz, warns: ‘You shut it down, they’re just going to create another.’

Meta claims it’s ‘working quickly’ on fixes, but for artists like Lloyd, the damage is visceral and personal. ‘It takes years to master an instrument and seconds to rip it off,’ she says. In the lawless frontier of AI content, your face, your talent, and your life’s work are just training data for the next viral bot—and your bank account is the next target.
