The Magic Kingdom’s tarnished crown just got a whole lot heavier, darlings. In a bombshell lawsuit that reads like the darkest, most twisted script Hollywood never wanted you to see, former Disney Channel star Raquel Lee Boileau has thrown open the dungeon doors on a nightmare she claims she endured at just 14 years old. And the villain, if her filings are to be believed? The House of Mouse itself.

In graphic, gut-wrenching detail, the actress from the 2001 flick 'Poof Point' alleges she was 'repeatedly raped, sexually assaulted, sexually abused and sexually harassed' by an adult male Disney employee on set. But here’s the real scandal simmering beneath Utah’ dusty surface: Lee claims Disney bigwigs knew. They saw. And in a move worthy of a corporate villain, they chose to look the other way, allegedly facilitating and concealing the predator’s actions. 'Disney’s set was not safe for children,' the lawsuit flatly declares, a chilling indictment of a brand built on pixie dust and family values.

Hold onto your Minnie ears, because the plot thickens like day-old churro batter. This isn’t Lee’s first rodeo with legal wranglers over the 'Poof Point' trauma. SalaciousNews has learned that back in 2015, the famed—and feared—attorney Gloria Allred brokered a hush-hush settlement for 'a small amount of money' with a male connected to the film. That confidential deal, sources whisper, is the very reason the alleged abuser is now cryptically named 'John Doe' in this new, multi-million-dollar pursuit. It’s the ultimate cover-up, unraveling in slow motion.

The suit doesn't stop there. It strategically name-drops the convicted monster Harvey Weinstein, alleging Disney has a 'longstanding and horrifying history of protecting sexual abusers.' They’re painting a picture of a corporate castle with a rotten moat, where child stars were left vulnerable to wolves in crew jackets. Even more jaw-dropping? Lee continued to work with Disney for years after, voicing roles in 'The Proud Family' revivals—a testament, her lawyers might argue, to the silencing power of trauma and nondisclosure agreements.

Why now? Blame California’s changed laws, honey. A 2020 measure gives survivors until their 40th birthday to file civil claims, and Lee, now nearly 40, is swinging the legal gavel with a vengeance. Her attorneys, former prosecutors, are promising a reckoning. 'This conduct is inexcusable,' they thunder, 'and those involved — directly or indirectly — must be held accountable.' Disney, for its part, is playing the silent castle, offering 'no comment' to the allegations. But in the court of public opinion, the verdict is already trending. The most magical place on earth just got a whole lot more sinister.
