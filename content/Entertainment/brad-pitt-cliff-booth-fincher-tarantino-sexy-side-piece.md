Forget the Oscars, the real trophies are the sparks flying between Brad Pitt and Elizabeth Debicki in the scandalously good sequel that’s got Hollywood buzzing like a beehive of gossip. ‘The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth’ isn’t just a movie; it’s a full-blown cinematic affair, a deliciously ‘salty and slower-burning side piece’ to Quentin Tarantino’s original masterpiece, according to insiders who’ve seen it. And darling, the affair is getting five-star reviews.

David Fincher, stepping in for the supposedly-retired Tarantino, has crafted what critics are breathlessly calling a ‘wildly entertaining’ ‘hangout movie’ that ‘pops, throbs and crackles.’ But let’s be honest, SalaciousNews readers: we’re not here for the cinematography. We’re here for the subtext. Descriptions of the film drip with innuendo: it’s ‘slick and sexy,’ with Debicki bringing an ‘intoxicating sensuality’ that has us wondering exactly what Cliff Booth is fixing for that ‘popular movie actor’ he works for. It’s a ‘ragtag’ cast in a ‘stylish love letter’ that’s less a sonnet and more a steamy late-night telegram.

The film, which sees Pitt slide back into his Oscar-winning role as the effortlessly cool stuntman-turned-fixer, is already being hailed as his best performance since the original. But the real drama might be behind the camera. Tarantino penned a monstrous 230-page script—practically a manifesto—which the notoriously meticulous Fincher then slashed by 60 pages. The creative tension between Tarantino’s ‘signature chaos’ and Fincher’s ‘meticulous attention to detail’ is the stuff of Hollywood legend, and the result, they say, is ‘peanut butter and chocolate’—if the peanut butter was ‘profane and the chocolate dipped in blood.’ We’re booking our IMAX tickets just for that description alone.

Is this the movie that will save Netflix’s prestige film ambitions? With a ‘fire final act’ and a cast that includes scene-stealers like Scott Caan and Peter Weller, who ‘could have had triple the screen time,’ it’s certainly the streaming giant’s hottest property this season. It’s a ‘pure cinematic trifecta’ and an ‘absolute banger’ that proves sometimes the sequel—like a good scandal—can be even juicier than the original. Just don’t tell Quentin we said that.
