Dreamhouse drama! The pink-hued paradise of Barbieland has been plunged into chaos by a cold, hard reality: cold, hard cash. SalaciousNews can exclusively reveal that the hotly-anticipated 'Barbie' sequel is on the brink of collapse after a vicious behind-the-scenes brawl over profits between Warner Bros. brass and the film's A-list creators. Talks have completely broken down, with studio co-heads Pam Abdy and Michael De Luca issuing a terse, defeated statement admitting, "we have been unable to reach an agreement."

Our Tinseltown moles whisper the fight centers on an explosive profit-sharing deal offered to superstars Margot Robbie and Ryan Gosling, and genius director Greta Gerwig. The trio, whose alchemy created a $1.5 billion global phenomenon, were reportedly offered a slice of the sequel's profits if it hits certain box office milestones—a standard sweetener for powerhouse talent. But the deal hit a wall of corporate greed when mega-boss David Zaslav got a look at the numbers and reportedly deemed it "too generous," putting the brakes on the entire project.

Warner Bros., in a classic Hollywood he-said-she-said, fired back, claiming the talent's reps turned down an offer back in May and haven't bothered to counter. But insiders sniff that this is a desperate PR move. The real ticking time bomb? Mattel holds the ultimate power. If a deal isn't signed by December, the rights to make any 'Barbie' sequel snap back to the toy titan, leaving Warner Bros. empty-handed and facing a shareholder nightmare.

The stalemate is a spectacular fall from the glittering highs of 2023's 'Barbenheimer' summer, where 'Barbie' wasn't just a movie but a cultural reset. Now, the future of the franchise hangs by a thread. Gerwig and co-writer Noah Baumbach have reportedly had a sequel idea for years, but without the star power of Robbie and Gosling—or Gerwig's visionary direction—a follow-up film would be dead on arrival. The message from Barbieland to the boardroom is clear: you can't put a price on magic, but you'd better be willing to pay for it.
