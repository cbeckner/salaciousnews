Step aside, Hollywood's latest feud—the real family drama is brewing in the most pious of places. Anna Faris, the ever-bubbly comedic force, has pulled back the curtain on her most intimate battle: a theological tug-of-war with her own 14-year-old son, Jack. And the ghost at the dinner table? None other than her famously devout ex, Chris Pratt.

In a bombshell revelation on Chelsea Handler's podcast, Faris confessed that her teenage son, whom she shares with the 'Jurassic World' star, is not just religious, but staunchly pro-life—a direct reflection of Pratt's outspoken Christian values. 'He’s asked me to accept Jesus Christ [as my savior],' Faris disclosed, painting a picture of dinner-table debates that sound more like seminary seminars. 'And I told him I would do anything for him, including this, but it’s going to take a whole lot of long conversations.'

The subtext is as scandalous as it gets: a celebrity divorce echoing through the soul of their child. While Pratt found renewed faith in the crucible of Jack's traumatic premature birth and subsequent health scares—vowing to 'devote my life to sharing God’s message'—Faris admits she 'didn’t grow up with religion.' Now, she's leveraging her trademark wit to 'challenge' her son's burgeoning beliefs. 'I’m like, ‘Do you think it’s a luxury that you get to be pro-life? Do you think that’s kind of a luxury?’’ she probes. His tentative reply? 'Maybe.'

Insiders whisper that this spiritual divide is the unspoken fault line in their co-parenting arrangement. Pratt, now married to church-going Katherine Schwarzenegger and raising a new brood in the faith, represents one pole. Faris, who married cinematographer Michael Barrett and is now a stepmom, represents another, more secular world. Their son Jack is caught in the middle, a 'solid as a tugboat' teenager navigating the choppy waters of parental ideology.

Faris claims a small victory by exposing Jack to the 'hypocrisy' of 'mega pastors that are kind of a grifter,' to which he apparently agrees they're 'the worst.' 'Okay, he’s getting there,' she mused. But the question lingers: Is this healthy questioning or the first act in a lifelong family schism? As Pratt once famously retorted to critics of his faith, quoting scripture, '2,000 years ago they hated him [Jesus], too.' One can only imagine the text thread.
