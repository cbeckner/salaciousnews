While the rain poured down on Sunday, so too did the fortunes—and the spirits—of the so-called ‘meme coin elite.’ A damp, dwindling gaggle of true believers huddled outside Trump National Golf Club in Virgina, clutching their digital wallets as their once-soaring $TRUMP tokens sank to a pathetic $0.88. This wasn't the glitzy, high-roller gala of yesteryear; this was the political equivalent of a going-out-of-business sale, with the President of the United States as the desperate auctioneer.

Insiders whispered the scene inside was ‘subdued,’ a diplomatic term for a funeral for failed fortunes. The crowd was smaller, the wallets were lighter, and the promised luminaries—like ‘Shark Tank’s’ Kevin O’Leary and boxer Floyd Mayweather—were nowhere to be seen offering a life raft. This marked the third such ‘exclusive’ dinner, a series that began with a staggering $148 million on the line and has now fizzled to a paltry $8.6 million. The only thing soaring here seems to be the ethical stench.

Democrats are howling, branding the entire spectacle a blatant ‘pay-for-access’ scheme dressed in the tattered rags of patriotism. ‘I’m doing it for the United States,’ Trump reportedly told the soggy attendees, a line that rings hollow when his own financial disclosures reveal he personally pocketed a cool $636 million from the very coin he’s peddling. It’s a masterclass in grift: convince the masses you’re fighting China, while you’re really just fighting to keep your own ledger in the black.

With Democrats now controlling Congress, the whispers of investigations are becoming deafening. Subpoenas are being sharpened like knives, aimed directly at this brazen fusion of the Oval Office and a crypto pump-and-dump scheme. The White House insists it was merely a ‘private event,’ but when the host is the Commander-in-Chief and the entry fee is ownership of his personal-branded financial product, the line between private and profoundly problematic evaporates faster than the value of a meme coin.

What remains is a stark portrait of ambition and decline. The coin launched to fanfare and $74 valuations on the eve of his second inauguration. Now, it’s worth less than a dollar, and its biggest holders are left standing in the rain, waiting for a photo-op with a president who has already banked their investment. The dream of being the ‘crypto capital of the world’ looks a lot like a deserted banquet hall after the last disappointed guest has gone home.
