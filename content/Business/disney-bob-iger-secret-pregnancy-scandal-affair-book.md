Pull up a chair, darlings, because the Magic Kingdom’s castle is built on sand—and scandal. A bombshell new biography is set to drop the curtain on Hollywood’s most powerful wizard, former Disney CEO Bob Iger, revealing a clandestine affair so messy it makes any Disney villain look tame. According to 'The House of Mouse' by Robbie Whelan, the corporate titan wasn’t just busy greenlighting blockbusters in the early 1990s; he was conducting a torrid extramarital liaison with an ABC marketer that resulted in a pregnancy.

Sources whisper that the woman, whose identity remains shrouded, endured a 'traumatic termination procedure,' a devastating personal chapter starkly at odds with Iger’s later public, polished championing of the #MeToo movement. The image of the impeccably suited executive, the man who brought you family-friendly fare for decades, now bears the shadow of a personal life that was anything but a fairy tale. Did the man who built an empire on ‘happily ever after’ leave a trail of heartbreak in his rise to the top?

But the dirt doesn’t stop at the bedroom door. The book paints a portrait of a mogul equally obsessed with power and paychecks. Despite raking in tens of millions annually, Iger allegedly seethed over his compensation. In a private 2014 email to Marvel bigwig Ike Perlmutter, he reportedly whined, 'I am frustrated about compensation! I had a nasty and distasteful negotiation.' Cue the world’s smallest violin for the executive who took home $46.5 million that year—a 36 percent bump. The tale suggests that behind the serene, sweater-vested facade was a man calculating every last zero.

The drama extends beyond Iger’s office. The book also claims his successor, Bob Chapek, looked upon the very fans who built Disney’s fortune with utter contempt. At a Marvel premiere, Chapek allegedly gestured toward devoted cosplayers and sneered, 'Did you get a look at the freaks?' It seems the House of Mouse has been home to more than a few rotten apples at the top.

Neither Iger nor Chapek responded to the salacious claims, leaving the book’s revelations hanging in the air like a dark curse over Cinderella’s Castle. As the December 1 release date looms, one thing is clear: the most compelling Disney story this season won’t be in a theater. It’s the one where the king is revealed to be all too human—and his kingdom, built on secrets now spilling into the light.
